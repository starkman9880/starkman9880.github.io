01 / Business problem

Separate operational patterns from gaps in the evidence

Shift reports can record the same condition repeatedly. Missing reports, inconsistent categories and incomplete times make comparisons difficult. I built a four-page Power BI report that shows recorded activity alongside reporting coverage and duration eligibility.

My Fire Life Safety Director experience informed the distinctions between a recorded observation, a unique incident and a confirmed restoration.

Monthly activity

Compare recorded impairments by month, category, floor and reason.

Recurring conditions

Explore trouble and supervisory sightings without counting every sighting as a new incident.

Evidence quality

Keep missing reports, uncertain times and excluded durations visible.

02 / Preparation and model

Four preparation tables, one shared calendar

Excel preparation→Power Query→Shared date model→DAX measures→Power BI report

The internal workflow preserves fixed record IDs and original labels alongside standardized categories. It flags uncertain dates, possible repeats, incomplete times and unresolved mappings. Impairments, FACP observations, alarm records and coverage connect to a date table through one-to-many relationships.

Directly observed conditions are separated from context and assumed-live rows. Durations are classified as eligible, requiring review or excluded. Only eligible values enter the average and median measures.

The public workbook was generated independently using the same table structure. It contains fictional records rather than anonymized operational rows.

04 / Analytical decisions

Make each measure's scope explicit

Observations are not incidents

The same condition may appear in several shifts. The report counts sightings without claiming a new incident each time.

Missing reports are not quiet days

Coverage is reported separately. No report does not establish that no event occurred.

Missing durations stay missing

Review and excluded records do not become zeros. Eligible counts accompany duration comparisons.

Average and median together

Comparing both measures helps expose the effect of long durations. Neither establishes total downtime.

05 / Validation and limitations

Report behavior checked; source review remains open

Record totals and duration classifications were checked, date filters and interactions tested, and Power Query conversion errors resolved while preserving source text. Data and schema refresh completed without reported errors. The separate synthetic report's headline totals were checked against its generated workbook.

Internal source limitations: January coverage is limited and October is partial. Recording practices changed in August 2026, limiting comparisons across that change. These historical limitations apply to the internal data, not the fictional demonstration.

Open source-review questions mean the internal analysis remains provisional. Repeated observations, incomplete time evidence and overlapping durations prevent claims of unique outage counts, total downtime or overall mean time to repair.

Operational records, the original Excel workbook and the internal PBIX are not distributed on this page. Synthetic results are demonstrations, not findings about a real building.

06 / Intended management use

Support focused investigation and better documentation

The report is designed to help teams identify recurring conditions, compare floor activity, locate documentation gaps and prioritize source review. It does not claim a measured reduction in downtime, response time or operational risk.

Skills demonstrated: Excel preparation, Power Query transformations, dimensional modeling, DAX, interactive Power BI reporting and analytical communication.
